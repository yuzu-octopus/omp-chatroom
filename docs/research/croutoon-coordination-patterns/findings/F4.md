## Conflicting evidence
- Linda many-writer vs single-writer lock: tuple space inherently multi-writer; imposing exclusion needs extra protocol, conflicts with "only lock holder writes" if that rule means no concurrent out.
- CRDT multi-writer convergence vs single-writer exclusion: CRDTs by design accept concurrent writes and converge via merge; vector clock + tie-breaker does not violate convergence but violates invariant that only one writer edits at a time and that causal history linearizes.
- Pubsub multi-publisher vs single-writer: NATS/MQTT explicit no cross-publisher total order; adding seq no per producer or central sequencer needed, conflicts with single lock unless lock is the sequencer.
- A2A/MCP opaque states vs shared chatroom log: A2A deliberately avoids shared internal state; a central chatroom store re-centralizes what A2A leaves decentralized, trade-off between auditability and autonomy.
- LWW wall-clock vs vector-clock ordering: sources disagree on "last"; vector clock wins causality correctness, timestamp wins simplicity but loses skew safety.

Compliant design keeps multi-agent concurrency at proposal level and serializes commit through lock holder that owns chatroom log; vector clocks may annotate turn metadata but cannot replace lock for convergence guarantee.
