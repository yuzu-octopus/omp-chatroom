# F7 — Retrieval vs classification suitability (one model or two?)

Angle: which models win at **short-text classification and dedup** versus **long-text retrieval**, and
whether a single model can serve both jobs. This decides the room's architecture (one embedding model
or two).

Fetch date for every item below: **2026-10-07** unless stated otherwise.

## Angle

The room has three embedding-shaped jobs (`docs/research/embeddings/brief.md` §"Existing context"):
(a) semantic retrieval over the log, (b) routing/classifying a message to a topic or tree node,
(c) near-duplicate post detection. The question is whether (a) and (b)+(c) want different models.

Three distinct claims are in play, and they must not be conflated:

1. **Cross-task rank divergence** — the model that wins retrieval is not the model that wins
   classification. This is a *measured* property of the benchmark field.
2. **Task-conditioning** — a single model can produce different embeddings for different jobs when
   the caller supplies a task type/instruction. This is a *design* property offered by vendors.
3. **Symmetry** — retrieval is asymmetric (short query → long document); classification and dedup are
   symmetric (text → text of comparable form). This is a *task-structure* property and is the
   mechanism behind (1) and (2).

The evidence supports: **one model is sufficient in principle (2) if and only if it exposes task
conditioning (2); without task conditioning, (1) means a retrieval-tuned model loses on
classification/dedup and vice versa.** For this repo the practical consequence is that the
architecture decision is not "one model or two" but "does the chosen model expose a task-type switch".

## Claims

### A. No single model dominates across tasks (cross-task rank divergence)

- **No particular text embedding method dominates across all tasks.** MTEB benchmarked 33 models on 58
  datasets / 8 tasks and states: "We find that no particular text embedding method dominates across all
  tasks. This suggests that the field has yet to converge on a universal text embedding method and scale
  it up sufficiently to provide state-of-the-art results on all embedding tasks." / https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07316 (abstract, v3) and https://arxiv.org/html/2210.07316v3 §4.2 / primary
- **"No model claims the state-of-the-art in all seven English tasks."** / https://arxiv.org/html/2210.07316v3 §4.2 / primary
- **Classification is won by a different family than retrieval, in the same benchmark run.** MTEB §4.2:
  "ST5 models dominate the classification task across most datasets… ST5-XXL has the highest average
  performance, 3% ahead of the best non-ST5 model, OpenAI Ada Similarity." / primary
- **The retrieval winners are different models.** MTEB §4.2: "SGPT-5.8B-msmarco is the best embedding
  model on the BEIR subset in MTEB as well as on the full BEIR benchmark." / primary
- **The divergence is explicit and directional:** MTEB §4.2: "Models geared towards STS (SimCSE, ST5,
  SGPT-nli) perform badly on retrieval tasks." and "Retrieval models (GTR, SGPT-msmarco) perform badly
  on STS." / primary
- **MTEB Table 1 (English subsets, main metric per task, %)** — the same row wins classification and
  loses retrieval, and vice versa / https://arxiv.org/html/2210.07316v3 Table 1 / primary:

  | Model | Class. | Clust. | PairClass. | Rerank. | Retr. | STS | Summ. | Avg. |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | ST5-XXL | **73.42** | 43.71 | 85.06 | 56.43 | 42.24 | **82.63** | 30.08 | 59.51 |
  | SGPT-5.8B-msmarco | 68.13 | 40.35 | 82.00 | 56.56 | **50.25** | 78.10 | 24.75 | 58.81 |
  | GTR-XXL | 67.41 | 42.42 | **86.12** | 56.65 | 48.48 | 78.38 | 30.64 | **58.97** |
  | MPNet | 65.07 | **43.69** | 83.04 | **59.36** | 43.81 | 80.28 | 27.49 | 57.78 |
  | MiniLM-L6 | 63.06 | 42.35 | 82.37 | 58.04 | 41.95 | 78.90 | 30.81 | 56.26 |

  Note the ST5-XXL vs SGPT-5.8B-msmarco inversion: 73.42 vs 68.13 on classification (ST5 +5.3), 42.24 vs
  50.25 on retrieval (SGPT +8.0). Neither wins both.
- **The mechanism is stated: retrieval is asymmetric, the other tasks are symmetric.** MTEB §4.2:
  "Retrieval tasks are unique in that there are two distinct types of texts: Queries and documents
  ('asymmetric'), while other tasks only have a single type of text ('symmetric'). On the QuoraRetrieval
  dataset, which has been shown to be largely symmetric, the playing field is more even with
  SGPT-5.8B-nli outperforming SGPT-5.8B-msmarco." / primary
- **The benchmark authors warn against reusing a model across framings.** MTEB §4.2: "Pair
  classification is closest to STS in its framing, yet models rank significantly differently on the two
  tasks. This highlights the importance of benchmarking on a diverse set of tasks to avoid blindly
  reusing a model for a different task." / primary
- **STS does not predict other tasks.** MTEB §1: "STS is known to poorly correlate with other real-world
  use cases." / primary
- **Restated in the 2025 multilingual successor (MMTEB), 132-task multilingual benchmark.** Per-category
  winners differ: `multilingual-e5-large-instruct` is rank 1 on MTEB(Multilingual) (Borda 1375) and
  MTEB(Indic) (209), while `GritLM-7B` is rank 1 on MTEB(Europe) (757). / https://arxiv.org/html/2502.13595v4 §3.3 Table 2 / primary
- **MMTEB per-category table shows category-level winners inside one model's own row set.**
  MTEB(Multilingual), 132 tasks — `multilingual-e5-large-instruct` leads Retrieval (57.1) and
  Classification (64.9), while `GritLM-7B` leads Reranking (63.8) and `e5-mistral-7b-instruct` leads
  Pair Classification (81.1). / same source / primary
- **An independent 2026 language-specific MTEB extension measured the split directly.**
  MTEB-BR (93 models, 22 native Brazilian-Portuguese tasks): "No single model leads all of retrieval,
  classification, and clustering, and the clustering tasks separate models weakly." / https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04581v2 §VI / primary
- **MTEB-BR "classification mirror image": the models that lose retrieval win classification.**
  "The same masked-language-model encoders that trail on retrieval rank among the leading models on
  classification, where MTEB fits a logistic-regression probe on frozen embeddings and so rewards
  linearly separable features rather than metric geometry. Averaged over the four classification tasks,
  the 335M BERTimbau-large ranks 3rd of 93 (second among open-weight models, behind only the 27B
  harrier-oss) and the 110M BERTimbau-base 9th, both ahead of every open decoder-LLM embedder except
  harrier and of most closed APIs, though neither was fine-tuned for embeddings." / same source / primary
- **Quantified objective effect on MTEB-BR retrieval+reranking.** "The 16 Portuguese-specific encoders
  average 0.331 across these eight tasks against 0.517 for the 12 general multilingual encoders, a
  0.186-point gap (Welch's t-test t=-3.45…)." The paper attributes this to training objective, not
  language: "retrieval-tuned encoders on the same language match the multilingual models, while
  masked-language-model and legal-domain encoders trail… the mirror-image strength of those same
  encoders on classification shows that model choice should follow the task." / same source §VI, §X / primary
- **MTEB-BR task discrimination (IRT `a_t`, 2-PL, 93-model panel): retrieval tasks separate models
  sharply, classification probes often do not.** Top six by discrimination are all retrieval
  (MedPTRetr 2.048 [1.89, 2.17], FaqBacen 1.876, Quati 1.784, FaQuADIR 1.761, BRTaxQAR 1.574,
  JurisTCU 1.486). Bottom: PortuLexRRI 0.505 [0.44, 0.60], Factck 0.467 [0.37, 0.58]. / same source §VIII Table IV / primary
- **MTEB-BR cross-category rank transfer from the global multilingual leaderboard is uneven.**
  Spearman ρ between HF MTEB multilingual rank and MTEB-BR rank (n=55 matched models, HF snapshot
  2026-06-29): overall 0.754; by category STS 0.80, reranking 0.75, retrieval 0.74, pair classification
  0.73, classification 0.68, clustering 0.62. / same source §VII / primary

### B. Symmetry vs asymmetry is the structural reason, and it is a first-party API concept

- **Cohere exposes a per-call task type covering exactly this split.** `input_type` ∈
  `search_query` / `search_document` for semantic search, and "When using embedding for `classification`
  and `clustering` tasks, you can set `input_type` to either 'classification' or 'clustering' to
  optimize the embeddings appropriately." / https://docs.cohere.com/docs/embeddings / primary
- **Gemini exposes the same split as named task types**, with duplicate detection explicitly assigned to
  the similarity type: `SEMANTIC_SIMILARITY` "Recommendation systems, duplicate detection";
  `CLASSIFICATION` "Sentiment analysis, spam detection"; `CLUSTERING` "Document organization, market
  research, anomaly detection"; `RETRIEVAL_DOCUMENT` / `RETRIEVAL_QUERY` for search. / https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/embeddings (Supported task types table) / primary
- **Gemini 2 moved task types into the prompt string**, and documents the asymmetric vs symmetric
  formatting explicitly: retrieval uses `task: search result | query: {content}` for the query and
  `title: {title} | text: {content}` for the document; classification uses
  `task: classification | query: {content}` for both sides. / same source / primary
- **EmbeddingGemma (300M, Gemma 3 text encoder) ships the same prompt taxonomy in its official model
  card**, with Semantic Similarity explicitly flagged "This is not intended for retrieval use cases."
  Prompt table: Retrieval (Query) `task: search result | query: {content}`; Retrieval (Document)
  `title: {title | "none"} | text: {content}`; Classification `task: classification | query: {content}`;
  Clustering `task: clustering | query: {content}`; Semantic Similarity
  `task: sentence similarity | query: {content}`. / https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/embeddinggemma/model_card / primary
- **Voyage's `input_type` covers only query/document** (`None` | `query` | `document`), i.e. Voyage's
  current 4-series has no first-class classification/clustering mode; its FAQ points classification and
  clustering users at a separate instruction list for the older `voyage-large-2-instruct` model.
  / https://docs.voyageai.com/docs/embeddings and https://docs.voyageai.com/docs/faq / primary
- **Qwen3-Embedding is instruction-aware and the vendor quantifies the benefit:** "Instruction Aware"
  is a documented model property for all three sizes; "Our evaluation indicates that, for most
  downstream tasks, using instructions (instruct) typically yields an improvement of 1% to 5% compared
  to not using them." / https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B / primary
- **sentence-transformers documents the same split in its API**, telling callers to use the
  task-specific encode methods for IR and the generic one otherwise: "If you are unsure whether you
  should use `encode()`, `encode_query()`, or `encode_document()`, your best bet is to use
  `encode_query()` and `encode_document()` for Information Retrieval tasks with clear query and
  document/passage distinction, and use `encode()` for all other tasks." / https://sbert.net/docs/package_reference/sentence_transformer/model.html / primary
- **BGE v1.5 documents that the query instruction is a retrieval-only affordance.** "If you need to
  search the relevant passages to a query, we suggest to add the instruction to the query; in other
  cases, no instruction is needed, just use the original query directly. In all cases, no instruction
  needs to be added to passages." / https://huggingface.co/BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5 (README, FlagEmbedding model list footnote [1]) / primary

### C. A single model CAN cover both jobs — the vendor answers are single-model multi-task designs

- **jina-embeddings-v3 (570M) is explicitly one model with five task adapters spanning both jobs.**
  Architecture section: "jina-embeddings-v3 supports four tasks and implements five adapters to choose
  from: retrieval.query and retrieval.passage for query and passage embeddings in asymmetric retrieval
  tasks, separation for clustering and reranking tasks, classification for classification tasks, and
  text-matching for tasks involving semantic similarity, such as STS or symmetric retrieval."
  / https://arxiv.org/html/2409.10173v3 §3 / primary
- **jina's own task table names deduplication as the text-matching adapter's job.** task value
  `text-matching`: "Semantic text similarity, general symmetric retrieval, recommendation, finding
  similar items, deduplication". task value `classification`: "Text classification".
  / same source Table 2 / primary
- **jina's paper states why the split exists:** "Embeddings can be used for various downstream tasks,
  including clustering, retrieval, and classification, each requiring different interpretations of the
  representation space resulting in different similarity metrics." / same source §3 / primary
- **jina reports the single-model cost of the union is small.** The LoRA adapters "account for less than
  3% of the total parameters, adding minimal overhead" (572M with adapters vs 559M base).
  / same source §3 Table 1 / primary
- **jina measures a single model scoring well on both jobs simultaneously.** MTEB English averages:
  jina-embeddings-v3 65.52 overall, 82.58 Classification, 45.27 Clustering, 53.87 Retrieval, 85.80 STS
  — highest Classification Accuracy of the multilingual set it compared against (text-embedding-3-large
  75.45, multilingual-e5-large-instruct 77.56, Cohere-embed-multilingual-v3.0 76.01).
  / same source §5.2 Table 4 / primary
- **jina documents its own per-task adapter routing as the reference configuration** — i.e. the vendor's
  own published scores require task-conditioned embedding: "For jina-embeddings-v3, all classification
  and pair-classification tasks are evaluated using the classification adapter, all STS tasks and three
  retrieval tasks (ArguAna, CQADupstackRetrieval, and QuoraRetrieval) are evaluated using the text
  matching adapter, all other retrieval tasks are evaluated using the retrieval adapter, and all
  clustering and reranking tasks are evaluated using the separation adapter." / same source §5.2 / primary
- **BGE-M3 is a single model doing dense + sparse + multi-vector retrieval** (multi-functionality), i.e.
  one model, multiple retrieval functions. It does not claim classification/clustering adapters.
  / https://huggingface.co/BAAI/bge-m3 / primary
- **GritLM is the cross-domain single-model claim** — one model trained for both generation and
  embedding: "Current models only perform well at one or the other… GRIT matches training on only
  generative or embedding data, thus we can unify both at no performance loss." (7B; not CPU-viable on
  the target box.) / https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09906 / primary
- **E5's design intent is a single general-purpose model across retrieval, clustering and
  classification:** "E5 can be readily used as a general-purpose embedding model for any tasks requiring
  a single-vector representation of texts such as retrieval, clustering, and classification."
  / https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03533 / primary

### E. How dedup is actually measured (it is not a retrieval task)

- **MTEB places duplicate detection in Pair Classification, not Retrieval.** MTEB §3.2 Pair
  Classification: "A pair of text inputs is provided and a label needs to be assigned. Labels are
  typically binary variables denoting duplicate or paraphrase pairs. The two texts are embedded and
  their distance is computed with various metrics… Using the best binary threshold accuracy, average
  precision, f1, precision and recall are computed. The average precision score based on cosine
  similarity is the main metric." / https://arxiv.org/html/2210.07316v3 §3.2 / primary
- **The canonical dedup dataset in MTEB is a pair-classification task.** `SprintDuplicateQuestions` —
  "Duplicate questions from the Sprint community", dataset
  `mteb/sprintduplicatequestions-pairclassification`, category pair classification, score `max_ap`.
  Also `TwitterSemEval2015`, `TwitterURLCorpus` (same category).
  / https://docs.mteb.org/overview/available_tasks/pair-classification/ / primary
- **Duplicate-question ranking is also framed as Reranking in MTEB** — `AskUbuntuDupQuestions` is a
  reranking task (`map`, `mrr`) present in every model card's model-index. / e.g. https://huggingface.co/api/models/BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5 (model-index entry `MTEB AskUbuntuDupQuestions`, task type `Reranking`) / primary
- **Dedup is therefore scored by a thresholded pair decision (AP/F1) or a ranking metric, not by
  nDCG@10 over a corpus** — the metric the room's recall path would use. A model can be strong at one
  and not the other, which is exactly what MTEB §4.2 warns about for the STS/pair-classification pair.
- **Both first-party task taxonomies route dedup to the symmetric/similarity mode.** Gemini:
  `SEMANTIC_SIMILARITY` → "duplicate detection". EmbeddingGemma model card: "Semantic Similarity:
  Embeddings optimized to assess text similarity, such as recommendation systems and duplicate
  detection". jina-embeddings-v3: `text-matching` → "…deduplication". / sources in §B above / primary
- **Published dedup pipelines use general-purpose embedding models plus clustering, not a
  dedup-specialised model.** SemDeDup (Meta AI / FAIR, C4 and LAION) embeds with a general pre-trained
  model (CLIP for images, OPT for language), k-means clusters, and thresholds cosine within cluster:
  "we leverage pre-trained foundation models to compare data similarity in the learned embedding space
  rather than in input space… we cluster the embeddings into k clusters via k-means. Within each
  cluster, we compute all pairwise cosine similarities and set a threshold cosine similarity above
  which data pairs are considered semantic duplicates." Reported effect on C4 (text): removing semantic
  duplicates "beating prior SoTA deduplication while providing efficiency gains of 15%".
  / https://arxiv.org/html/2303.09540v3 §3, §1 / primary
- **NVIDIA NeMo Curator implements the same recipe in a shipping product** — `SemanticDeduplicationWorkflow`,
  embeddings + k-means, citing SemDeDup. Note it requires GPU for both stages, so it is not usable on
  the target CPU-only host as shipped. / https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo/curator/curate-text/process-data/deduplication/semdedup / primary

### F. Published guidance on model selection for mixed workloads

- **Do not select on the aggregate leaderboard number; select on the task that matches the workload.**
  Primary statements: MTEB §4.2 (no model dominates; different tasks have different winners);
  MTEB-BR §X ("model choice should follow the task"); MTEB-BR §VII (category-level rank transfer from
  the global leaderboard is 0.62–0.80, i.e. imperfect).
- **MTEB's own maintainers confirm the leaderboard's aggregate rank is not a per-task rank.** Borda
  points are accumulated per task; "For any score where the model is not evaluated, the model assumed
  gets a zero in the Borda count… a model with only retrieval results should not expect its overall rank
  to be the same as its retrieval rank… the retrieval/RTEB view is the more appropriate comparison
  surface." / https://github.com/embeddings-benchmark/mteb/discussions/3706 (Kenneth Enevoldsen,
  maintainer, 2025-12-10; GioiaZheng, 2026-09-11) / primary (maintainer statement on the official repo)
- **The maintainers state the design intent explicitly:** "not penalizing would encourage people to
  submit only scores for tasks on which they performed well… By making this decision, we implicitly
  encourage general-purpose models over specialized models." / same thread / primary
- **Retrieval now has its own leaderboard surface (RTEB) precisely because one rank cannot serve both.**
  RTEB is retrieval-only, NDCG@10 by default, with a hybrid open/private dataset split to defeat
  benchmark overfitting: "models with a lower zero-shot score may perform very well on the benchmark,
  without generalizing to new problems. For this reason, models with slightly lower benchmark
  performance and a higher zero-shot score are often recommended instead."
  / https://huggingface.co/blog/rteb (HuggingFace/MongoDB/MTEB, 2025-10-01) / primary
- **sentence-transformers' official guidance for the target-deployment question:** "Consider using the
  Massive Textual Embedding Benchmark leaderboard as an inspiration of strong Sentence Transformer
  models. Be wary: Model sizes: it is recommended to filter away the large models that might not be
  feasible without excessive hardware. Experimentation is key: models that perform well on the
  leaderboard do not necessarily do well on your tasks, it is crucial to experiment with various
  promising models." / https://sbert.net/docs/sentence_transformer/pretrained_models.html / primary
- **sentence-transformers documents the same shape of specialization in its own model catalogue** —
  general-purpose models vs dedicated Semantic Search (MS MARCO / 215M QA pairs) models vs STS models,
  each with its own published score column. / same source / primary

## Conflicting evidence

1. **EmbeddingGemma vs Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B on classification reverses between benchmarks.**
   EmbeddingGemma beats Qwen3-0.6B on MTEB(Eng v2) classification (87.6 vs 85.76) but loses on
   MTEB(Multilingual v2) classification (60.90 vs 66.83). Both numbers are primary and from the same
   snapshot family. Consequence: "which small model wins classification" is benchmark-dependent, so a
   one-model choice cannot be made on a single category table.
   / https://arxiv.org/html/2509.20354v3 Tables 6–7 vs https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B / primary
2. **EmbeddingGemma's MTEB(Eng v2) Mean(Task) appears as both 69.67 and 69.7** across the model card
   and the paper's Table 7. Same figure to 3 s.f.; rounding only, not a conflict in substance.
   / https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/embeddinggemma/model_card vs https://arxiv.org/html/2509.20354v3 Table 7 / primary
3. **jina-embeddings-v3 is cc-by-nc-4.0, not a permissive licence.** The HF API lists
   `license:cc-by-nc-4.0` (arXiv paper is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). Any recommendation built on jina-v3's
   single-model multi-task story must carry this constraint. MTEB-BR §IX independently flags the same
   class of problem: "several otherwise-competitive open models (Linq-Embed-Mistral, SFR-Embedding,
   Jina-v5) ship under non-commercial terms that bar production use, whereas Qwen3-Embedding is
   Apache-2.0." / https://huggingface.co/api/models/jinaai/jina-embeddings-v3; https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04581v2 §IX / primary
4. **jina-v3 MTEB(Multilingual v2) retrieval (55.76) is below BGE-M3 (54.60) and far below
   Qwen3-0.6B (64.64)**, despite jina-v3 being the canonical "one model, all tasks" design. A
   task-conditioned single model buys coverage, not necessarily the retrieval ceiling.
   / https://arxiv.org/html/2509.20354v3 Table 5 / primary
5. **"One model or two" is not answered uniformly by the vendors.** jina-v3, Gemini, Cohere and
   EmbeddingGemma all expose a task switch (→ one model, conditioned). Voyage's current 4-series exposes
   only query/document (→ retrieval-only conditioning; classification/clustering users are redirected to
   a different, older model). MTEB/MMTEB/MTEB-BR measure rank divergence (→ two models if you pick
   without conditioning). These are not contradictory, but a report that cites only one of them will
   give the wrong architecture advice. / sources in §B / primary
6. **RTEB has no arXiv paper.** A search for "RTEB Retrieval Embedding Benchmark MTEB arXiv 2025"
   returns an arXiv id that belongs to a different paper (`2506.21182` is the MTEB *maintenance* paper,
   not RTEB). The primary RTEB source is the HuggingFace blog announcement dated 2025-10-01; MTEB docs
   list RTEB as beta. Anyone citing "the RTEB paper" is citing something that does not exist.
   / https://huggingface.co/blog/rteb; https://docs.mteb.org/overview/available_benchmarks/ / primary
7. **Secondary guidance (tagged as such) states the same conclusion but adds no primary numbers.**
   Modal's "Top embedding models on the MTEB leaderboard" states: "a model tuned for retrieval and
   semantic textual similarity… may underperform on clustering or classification, which brings down the
   average. Conversely, a model that performs steadily across all tasks (but not exceptionally) can end
   up with a higher average while being weaker at retrieval. In other words, the best overall model is
   not always the top choice for your workload." / https://modal.com/blog/mteb-leaderboard-article /
   **secondary** — one extra search was run to locate the underlying primary; the claim is corroborated
   by the primary MTEB paper (§4.2) and MTEB-BR (§VI, §X), so the primary supersedes it.
   Similar secondary restatements (Vercel embedding-model-selection guide, CodeSOTA MTEB page, premai
   RAG ranking, nayakpplaban Medium post, emergentmind topic page, GeeksforGeeks MTEB page,
   mbrenndoerfer.com) were located and **not relied on**; no unique primary data appears in them.
   / **secondary**

## Gaps

- **No published benchmark isolates "short-text classification" and "long-text retrieval" as the two
  axes for the exact sizes in this repo's CPU budget.** MTEB(Eng v2) classification tasks are mostly
  short-text (Banking77, AmazonCounterfactual, MassiveIntent, ToxicConversations, TweetSentiment) and
  MTEB retrieval tasks are mostly short-query→short-passage (BEIR), with the long-document cases split
  into LongEmbed/BRIGHT long subsets that are not in the per-task-type tables extracted here. So the
  "short-text classification vs *long-text* retrieval" contrast is assembled from two benchmark families
  rather than measured in one run.
- **No published MTEB task-type score for a dedup-specific task per model at the CPU-relevant sizes.**
  SprintDuplicateQuestions / TwitterSemEval2015 / TwitterURLCorpus are pair-classification tasks, but
  no vendor publishes those per-task numbers in a card or paper located here; only aggregate PairClass.
  MTEB-BR's pair-classification tasks (AssinRTE, InferBR) are Portuguese-only.
- **No published guidance that says "use two embedding models" or "use one" as a direct
  recommendation** from a benchmark maintainer. The maintainers' nearest statement is the Borda
  zero-for-missing-tasks design intent (encouraging general-purpose models) plus the separate RTEB
  retrieval surface — both of which imply "one conditioned model" is acceptable and "one unconditioned
  model" is not.
- **Voyage's classification/clustering instructions are only documented for the legacy
  `voyage-large-2-instruct`** (a GitHub instructions file), not for the current 4-series; the FAQ says
  the 4-series "outperforms our domain-specific models" and gives no classification/clustering mode.
  Unverified whether 4-series embeddings are intended for classification at all.
- **No first-party CPU latency numbers attached to the classification-vs-retrieval decision.** This
  angle is about quality; the CPU cost side is F5/E3's scope. The interaction — e.g. whether running two
  models instead of one is affordable on the i5-1135G7 — is not answerable from any source found here.
- **MTEB-BR is Brazilian Portuguese and cannot be transplanted.** Its cross-category rank correlations
  (classification ρ=0.68, clustering ρ=0.62) are the best published *quantification* of the divergence
  located, but they are measured on 22 non-English tasks. The equivalent per-category correlation for
  English MTEB has not been published; MTEB's own paper reports only that no model dominates.
- **"Unverified": whether any single small CPU-feasible model has a published per-task-type score where
  it is simultaneously rank-1 in retrieval and rank-1 in classification.** No such row was found in
  MTEB(Eng v2), MTEB(Multi v2), or MTEB-BR.
