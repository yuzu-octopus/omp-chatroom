# F8 — Classic (non-LLM) MAS coordination theory: what the ORIGINAL papers establish

## Angle

F8 asks what the *primary* classic MAS literature (contract nets, blackboards, tuple spaces/Linda, stigmergy, market/bidding allocation) actually establishes about (a) which mechanism property prevents which coordination failure, (b) the deadlock/starvation conditions each mechanism is known to have *in the original's own words*, and (c) whether the originals discuss task-dependency ordering, ownership/handoff, and escalation — the three things a TTL-lease claim board must get right.

- Task-dependency ordering is a first-class field in the 1980 protocol: subcontract records carry `predecessors`/`successors`, and "A subcontract will not be announced … until its predecessors have been completed" — ordering is enforced by withholding the announcement (the claim primitive), not by a separate scheduler. → https://www.reidgsmith.com/The_Contract_Net_Protocol_Dec-1980.pdf / fetch 2026-10-20 / **primary**

- TTL-like semantics: the task announcement carries an **expiration time** = "a deadline for receiving bids"; late bids "at worst … result in a suboptimum selection of contractors"; the manager can reissue, wait, or execute the task itself. → https://www.reidgsmith.com/The_Contract_Net_Protocol_Dec-1980.pdf / fetch 2026-10-20 / **primary**

- Idle-node signalling has a classic inverse form: the **node-available** message lets an idle node advertise and be matched, "even in this case we have the mutual selection aspect" — precedent for a wait-for-work primitive. → https://www.reidgsmith.com/The_Contract_Net_Protocol_Dec-1980.pdf / fetch 2026-10-20 / **primary**

The quoted defaults are **primary**; the consequence (long-waiting tasks can starve unless aging is added) is my **inference** from them. → https://www.reidgsmith.com/The_Contract_Net_Protocol_Dec-1980.pdf / fetch 2026-10-20 / **primary** (quote) + **speculative** (starvation inference)

### B. Deadlock / starvation / livelock conditions, in the original's own words

- **Contract net — deadlock is caused by the *bidding* policy, not the protocol**: "Waiting for an accept-proposal or reject-proposal message before making any further bids will result in deadlocks, or timing out of the protocol on the side of the initiator." → **Eager Bidder Problem**: an agent that commits resources at bid time may get "any bid accepted while resources have been allocated while other negotiations have come to an end"; an agent that commits late "may have made bids for more tasks than its resources allow for, possibly all being accepted and resulting in commitments that cannot be kept." Three remedies: (i) leveled commitment (safe but many messages; worst case "only it requires 2n more messages"), (ii) protocol redesign, (iii) statistical. Under FIPA's CNP "in case a single agent fails, there is no danger of a deadlock" — but leveled commitment "allows for many messages being sent, the increased number of messages requires an equally …" cost. — Schillo, Kray & Fischer 2002, https://christian-kray.de/pub/2002_aamas.pdf
- **Contract net — formal verification says: no *unexpected* deadlock, no livelock, bounded messages, but only under restrictive assumptions** (single auctioneer, bounded bidders, no deadlines yet): dead markings number exactly MaxBdrs+1 and "All these MaxBdrs+1 dead markings represent **expected termination** of the protocol"; the system "exhibits cyclic behaviour, [but] it does not livelock … given suitable **fairness assumptions**"; channel bounded by MaxBdrs; "we would like to … relax Assumptions 1 and 4, by extending the model to open multi-agent systems and introducing deadlines." — Billington, Gupta & Gallasch 2008, https://dl.ifip.org/db/conf/forte/forte2008/BillingtonGG08.pdf
- **Contract net — starvation risk from the default ranking policy**: CNET's default announcement-ranking "give[s] the most recently received task announcement the highest ranking", and the default bid choice is "the most recently received task" because "older tasks are more likely to have been already awarded" — i.e. the original's *default* is recency-biased, so long-waiting tasks can be starved. — Smith 1980, https://www.reidgsmith.com/The_Contract_Net_Protocol_Dec-1980.pdf
- **Contract net — node failure is explicitly survivable but detection is the manager's job**: manager detects contractor failure and re-announces the contract to another node. — Smith 1980 (same URL)
- **Blackboard — the "blocking" failure is a *scheduling* failure, not a data failure**: HEARSAY-I's weaknesses were "the lock-step control sequence that limited 'parallelism'", "the lack of provision to express relationships among alternative sentence hypotheses", and "the built-in problem-solving strategy that made modifications awkward and comparisons of different strategies impossible". Termination in the blackboard framework is explicit: "Criteria are provided to determine when to terminate the process. Usually, one of the knowledge sources indicates when the problem-solving process is terminated, either because an acceptable solution has been found or because the system cannot continue further for lack of knowledge or data." — Nii 1986 Part I, https://ojs.aaai.org/aimagazine/index.php/aimagazine/article/download/537/473
- **Blackboard — centralization of control is an open research question, not a solved property**: "One of the major research issues in distributed knowledge-based systems is the distribution of control—how much and what kinds of global control are required, and how much control can remain local." — Nii 1986 Part II, https://ojs.aaai.org/aimagazine/index.php/aimagazine/article/download/550/486
- **Linda — deadlock does not vanish; it relocates and becomes detectable**: "A deadlock exists in the system iff there is a cycle in the access graph"; the Linda solution "detects deadlock by turning the set of transaction processes and lock processes into a life-size … transaction graph" with exchanged tokens — i.e. cycle detection, not cycle prevention. — Gelernter 1985, https://www.cs.unc.edu/~stotts/COMP590-059-f21/slides/lindaGenerative.pdf
- **Stigmergy — starvation is by construction (idle workers) and is only mitigated by slack**: "does not guarantee an optimal use of the 'workforce'"; fix = pool of available workers so "there would always be some workers available for any job that opens up, without requiring workers to wait long times in between jobs, or tasks to wait long for workers to execute them." — Heylighen 2016, https://pespmc1.vub.ac.be/Papers/StigmergyICognSystems.pdf
- **Shared-memory coordination (CSP line) — the failure mode is *blocking*, and it is the intended semantics**: the buffer example is the canonical statement: "New arrivals can occur only if there is space in the buffer; **if the buffer is full, arrivals will be blocked**." Shared memory "serves only to restrict the possible traces of a process"; it is "a modeling convenience, since it does not increase the descriptive power of FPs" — i.e. backpressure via shared memory is a *derived* property of the trace restriction, not a new mechanism. — Inan & Varaiya 1988, https://pure.iiasa.ac.at/3067/1/XB-88-401.pdf

### C. Task-dependency ordering, ownership/handoff, escalation — as the ORIGINALS handle them

- **Task-dependency ordering is a first-class field of the 1980 contract net.** The subcontract record carries `predecessors` and `successors`: "`predecessors` are the names of subcontracts that must be performed before this one can be addressed directly and therefore do not have to be [announced] … Predecessors and successors are necessary for tasks that must be performed in a particular order, such as those that occur in planning applications. **A subcontract will not be announced … until its predecessors have been completed.**" Ordering is enforced by *withholding announcement*, i.e. by the claim primitive, not by a separate scheduler. — Smith 1980, https://www.reidgsmith.com/The_Contract_Net_Protocol_Dec-1980.pdf
- **Ownership/handoff is explicit and layered in the 1980 protocol**: contract state machine READY → EXECUTING → ANNOUNCED (subcontract) → SUSPENDED → TERMINATED; a contractor "may further partition a task and award contracts to other nodes. It is then the manager for those contracts" (recursive handoff); report recipients and "related contractors" are first-class slots; interim/final reports and the **termination** message let a manager cancel work ("The contractor receiving such a message terminates execution of the contract indicated in the message and all of its outstanding subcontracts") — the original's cancel/abort primitive. — Smith 1980 (same URL)
- **Escalation/deadline semantics already exist as message types**: `immediate response bids` (INELIGIBLE / BUSY / LOW RANKING) = structured "why is this not moving" escalation; `directed award` + `acknowledgment`/`refusal` with a **refusal justification slot** ("INELIGIBLE" or "NO TEMPLATE") = refusal-with-reason, i.e. the original already distinguishes *cannot* from *will not* from *not now*. — Smith 1980 (same URL)
- **"Node available" inverts the negotiation** — an idle node advertises itself instead of waiting, and mutual selection is preserved ("even in this case we have the mutual selection aspect"). This is the classic precedent for a *wait-for-work* primitive. — Smith 1980 (same URL)
- **Smith's own efficiency criterion (the one the assignment asks for) is a speedup/efficiency curve, not a fairness criterion**: the 1980 Search paper computes required processors per time step, normalized speedup and "the decrease in efficiency (i.e., speedup per processor). **This is a conservative estimate of efficiency, in that it includes processors that stand idle near the start and end of the search.**" So Smith's criterion already prices *idle capacity* as a loss. — Smith 1980 (Search), https://www.reidgsmith.com/Applications_of_the_Contract_Net_Framework_-_Search_16-May-1980.pdf
- **The 1981 cooperation paper states the dependency assumption that distributed DPS breaks**: "It has generally been assumed … that a well-defined and *a priori* partitioned problem exists … **Complete knowledge of timing and precedence relations between tasks has generally been assumed**" — and distributed DPS instead makes partitioning part of the task; precedence must be *discovered/negotiated*, not assumed. — Smith & Davis 1981, https://www.reidgsmith.com/Frameworks_for_Cooperation_in_Distributed_Problem_Solving_Jan-1981.pdf
- **The blackboard analogue of "who is blocked on what" is the control component, not the data**: HEARSAY-II's control component was "a blackboard monitor and a scheduler"; "The monitor kept an account of each change made to the blackboard, its primitive change type, and any new hypotheses." — i.e. a *change-log + focus-of-attention* mechanism, the closest classic analogue to our digest + attention tiers. — Nii 1986 Part II, https://ojs.aaai.org/aimagazine/index.php/aimagazine/article/download/550/486
- **Over-sharing is also a documented failure** (relevant to our T2 chatter tier): in distributed HEARSAY-II, "remotely generated results sometimes caused nodes to pursue lines of attack that were not as fruitful as the ones they had been pursuing before receipt of those results. Some evidence of this phenomenon can be inferred from the experiments that were performed **in which some results were lost in transmission. In some cases, system performance actually improved**." — Smith & Davis 1981, https://www.reidgsmith.com/Frameworks_for_Cooperation_in_Distributed_Problem_Solving_Jan-1981.pdf
- **Canonical-primary corroboration for the collection** (bibliographic, Crossref-verified): *Readings in Distributed Artificial Intelligence* (Bond & Gasser 1988) reprints, in order, Smith & Davis "Frameworks for Cooperation" (pp. 61-70), Smith "The Contract Net Protocol" (pp. 357-366), Yang/Huhns/Stephens "An Architecture for Control and Communications in DAI Systems" (pp. 606-616), Lesser & Corkill "Functionally Accurate, Cooperative Distributed Systems" (pp. 295-310), Durfee/Lesser/Corkill "Coherent Cooperation Among Communicating Problem Solvers" (pp. 268-284), Rosenschein "Synchronization of Multi-Agent Plans" (pp. 187-191) — i.e. the canon itself treats *task-sharing, synchronization and functional accuracy under partial failure* as the DAI core. — https://api.crossref.org/works?filter=prefix:10.1016&query.container-title=Readings%20in%20Distributed%20Artificial%20Intelligence&rows=60

### D. Direct implications for OUR plugin (claim board with TTL leases)

- The 1980 contract net's TTL-like primitive is the **expiration time on an announcement** (bid deadline) plus the **reclaim path** (manager detects contractor failure → re-announce → re-award). Our TTL-lease + sweep + "oldest waiter promoted" maps onto that, and the original's explicit owner-failure path is a direct precedent for our reclaim-is-logged rule. (Smith 1980, primary.)
- The original's **dependency ordering is enforced by withholding the announcement** until predecessors complete — the closest classic precedent for "claim A blocks claim B" being enforced by the claim primitive rather than by a scheduler. (Smith 1980, primary.)
- The original's **structured refusal reasons** (INELIGIBLE / BUSY / LOW RANKING / NO TEMPLATE) are the precedent for a room that records *why* a claim did not move, i.e. for our missing `contended` record kind. (Smith 1980, primary.)
- **Smith's own efficiency criterion prices idle capacity as a loss** — evidence that "agents waiting because nobody claimed" should be visible as waste, not as normal operation. (Smith 1980 Search, primary.)
- The **blackboard's own historical failure** (too-coarse shared state ⇒ recomputation by every KS; lock-step scheduler ⇒ limited parallelism) is the strongest classic argument that a shared room with no ownership/ordering will (i) duplicate work and (ii) serialize. Both of our plugin's current weaknesses (no dependency ordering, no contention record) are historically documented blackboard failures, not novel risks. (Nii 1986 Parts I & II, primary.)
- **Stigmergy's lesson is about the medium, not the agents**: it needs a persistent, shared, readable state and it buys scale and fault-tolerance only when the medium outlives individual agents and no supervisor is required. Our append-only JSONL log + cold summary tree is a stigmergic medium; the classic says the payoff (scale, no supervisor bottleneck) requires that agents read the medium rather than negotiate, and that failure of one agent is automatically covered. (Heylighen 2016, primary.)
- **Classic auction literature warns fairness and efficiency pull apart**, so a claim board that is a pure efficiency allocator (first-claim-wins) inherits the documented fairness gap; de Weerdt et al. show efficient algorithms often cannot be made truthful/stable. (de Weerdt et al. 2012, primary.)
