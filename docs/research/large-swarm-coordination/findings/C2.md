### A. Classic mechanism → failure MAPPING (F8; what the ORIGINALS actually credit)

- **A1. Contract net's defining property is MUTUAL SELECTION, not central assignment.** "Final agreement is achieved by mutual selection"; manager/contractor are dynamic roles ("Individual nodes are not designated *a priori* as managers or contractors"); "there is neither global control nor global data storage". → https://www.reidgsmith.com/The_Contract_Net_Protocol_Dec-1980.pdf / primary `[F8]`
- **A2. The ORIGINAL names duplication-vs-starvation as the unsolved core.** "Mechanisms must be designed that give some assurance that individual subproblems are actually processed, that processors do not get in each other's way in trying to solve identical subproblems while other subproblems are inadvertently ignored… much work remains to be done." → https://www.reidgsmith.com/Frameworks_for_Cooperation_in_Distributed_Problem_Solving_Jan-1981.pdf / primary `[F8]`
- **A3. Task-dependency ordering is a FIRST-CLASS field of the 1980 protocol, enforced by withholding the announcement (the claim primitive), not by a scheduler.** Subcontract records carry `predecessors`/`successors`; "**A subcontract will not be announced … until its predecessors have been completed.**" → https://www.reidgsmith.com/The_Contract_Net_Protocol_Dec-1980.pdf / primary `[F8]`
- **A4. Ownership + handoff + cancel are already 1980 message types.** Recursive subcontracting ("It is then the manager for those contracts"); contract record slots `report-recipients` / `related-contractors`; contract state machine READY→EXECUTING→ANNOUNCED→SUSPENDED→TERMINATED; a **termination** message cancels "the contract indicated in the message and all of its outstanding subcontracts". → same URL / primary `[F8]`
- **A5. Escalation-with-reason pre-exists: cannot / will-not / not-now are distinguished.** Immediate-response bids carry reasons INELIGIBLE / BUSY / LOW RANKING; a directed award's refusal carries a **refusal justification** (INELIGIBLE / NO TEMPLATE). → same URL / primary `[F8]` — the classic precedent for a room record kind that says *why a claim did not move*.
- **A6. TTL precedent = announcement "expiration time" as a BID DEADLINE, plus an owner-driven reclaim path.** Late bids "at worst … result in a suboptimum selection of contractors"; "The failure of any contractor … can be detected by its manager; the contract … can then be re-announced and awarded to another node"; "There are no nodes whose failure can completely block the contract negotiation process." → same URL / primary `[F8]`
- **A7. Idle capacity is priced as LOSS in Smith's own efficiency criterion.** The 1980 *Search* paper's efficiency curve is speedup-per-processor and "**includes processors that stand idle near the start and end of the search**". → https://www.reidgsmith.com/Applications_of_the_Contract_Net_Framework_-_Search_16-May-1980.pdf / primary `[F8]` — waiting-for-work must be visible as waste, not as normal operation.
- **A8. Contract-net DEADLOCK comes from the BIDDING POLICY, not the protocol.** "Waiting for an accept-proposal or reject-proposal message before making any further bids will result in deadlocks, or timing out of the protocol on the side of the initiator." Eager bidding commits resources for work that will not be awarded; late bidding commits to more tasks than resources allow. Leveled commitment is safe but "only it requires 2n more messages". → https://christian-kray.de/pub/2002_aamas.pdf / primary `[F8]`
- **A9. The formal CNP guarantee is NARROW — do not quote it as a theorem.** Billington/Gupta/Gallasch (CPN): no *unexpected* deadlock (dead markings = MaxBdrs+1, "All these … represent **expected termination**"), no livelock "given suitable **fairness assumptions**", channel bounded by MaxBdrs — but for **one** auctioneer, ≤5 bidders, and "introducing deadlines" is future work. → https://dl.ifip.org/db/conf/forte/forte2008/BillingtonGG08.pdf / primary `[F8]`
- **A10. Starvation risk is in the DEFAULT ranking policy.** CNET's default announcement-ranking gives "the most recently received task announcement the highest ranking", and the default bid choice is "the most recently received task" because "older tasks are more likely to have been already awarded". The quotes are primary; the starvation consequence is the worker's **inference**. → same URL as A1 / primary (quote) + speculative (inference) `[F8]`
- **A11. Blackboard model specifies NO control component — control must be added, and the classic analogy is the serialization bottleneck.** "There is no control component specified in the blackboard model. The actual locus of control can be in the knowledge sources, on the blackboard, in a separate module, or in some combination of the three."; the jigsaw analogy: "one center aisle … no more than one person can go up to the blackboard at one time, and a monitor is needed … to choose the order". → https://ojs.aaai.org/aimagazine/index.php/aimagazine/article/download/537/473 / primary `[F8]`
- **A12. Blackboard's OWN historical failures are (i) duplicate work and (ii) serialization.** HEARSAY-I's word-level-only blackboard meant "the inability to share information contributed by nonword knowledge sources caused the information to be **recomputed by each knowledge source**"; and its "lock-step control sequence that **limited 'parallelism'**". Also "the lack of provision to express relationships among alternative … hypotheses". → same URL / primary `[F8]`
- **A13. Blackboard's data-structure lesson is the addressing-fragility fix.** "The message-passing paradigm requires a recipient of a message as well as a sender. Often, the recipient of the message is not known, or the recipient might have been deleted." (HEARSAY-II's message passing) → https://ojs.aaai.org/aimagazine/index.php/aimagazine/article/download/550/486 / primary `[F8]`
- **A14. Blackboard's monitor is the closest classic analogue to our digest + attention tiers.** HEARSAY-II had "a blackboard monitor and a scheduler"; "The monitor kept an account of each change made to the blackboard, its primitive change type, and any new hypotheses." → same URL as A13 / primary `[F8]`
- **A15. Blackboard mis-partition is a documented slow failure.** "If the initial organization of the blackboard data … is wrong, then modifications result in a rapid deterioration of the system structure … a clever programmer can go a long way to make things work, even knowing that things will get progressively worse as more knowledge is added." → same URL as A13 / primary `[F8]`
- **A16. Tuple space buys address-free, time-decoupled communication but relocates rather than removes deadlock.** "a tuple in TS is equally accessible to all processes within TS, but is bound to none"; semantic mismatch → a non-matching tuple "will be rejected and must be retransmitted"; "Blocked requests, and consequently blocked processes and blocked-process queues, are the domain of the kernel"; "A deadlock exists in the system iff there is a **cycle in the access graph**" (detection, not prevention); efficiency never demonstrated ("Generative communication might place unacceptable burdens on network communication systems"). → https://www.cs.unc.edu/~stotts/COMP590-059-f21/slides/lindaGenerative.pdf / primary `[F8]`
- **A17. Stigmergy's guarantees are properties of the MEDIUM, not of the agents.** "actions are performed automatically in the right order" with "no planning or anticipation", "no memory", "no communication", "no mutual awareness", "no simultaneous presence"; "It also does not depend on the number of agents, tasks, or dependencies between tasks." Requirements: agents can recognize triggering conditions and all can access the medium. → https://pespmc1.vub.ac.be/Papers/StigmergyICognSystems.pdf / primary `[F8]`
- **A18. Stigmergy's costs and its explicit anti-supervisor claim.** "the stigmergic approach does not guarantee an **optimal use of the 'workforce'**" (idle workers; fix = pool of available workers / massive parallelism); a supervisor alternative "becomes a bottleneck that is even more sensitive to disturbances, creating the risk that the whole plan falls apart because the supervisor is not available to pass on reschedules"; a quitting agent "is automatically replaced by another one". → same URL / primary `[F8]`
- **A19. Fairness ≠ efficiency in auction allocation.** Efficient algorithms often cannot be made truthful — "the efficient greedy algorithm cannot be used in a truthful mechanism, therefore an alternative, cluster-based algorithm is proposed"; revenue-sharing strategies carry "no theoretical guarantees on stability or incentive compatibility"; multi-round auctions land "experimentally close to optimal allocations" but scale better than optimal solvers. → https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10458-011-9168-3.pdf , https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2014/aamas/p1443.pdf / primary `[F8]`
- **A20. Over-sharing is a documented failure (relevant to our T2 chatter tier).** In distributed HEARSAY-II, "remotely generated results sometimes caused nodes to pursue lines of attack that were not as fruitful"; and "some results were lost in transmission. **In some cases, system performance actually improved**." → https://www.reidgsmith.com/Frameworks_for_Cooperation_in_Distributed_Problem_Solving_Jan-1981.pdf / primary `[F8]`
- **A21. Distributed DPS breaks the classical precedence assumption.** "**Complete knowledge of timing and precedence relations between tasks has generally been assumed**" — in distributed DPS, partitioning is part of the problem, so precedence must be *negotiated*, not assumed. → same URL / primary `[F8]`
- **A22. Shared-memory (CSP-line) coordination makes BLOCKING the intended semantics.** "New arrivals can occur only if there is space in the buffer; **if the buffer is full, arrivals will be blocked**"; shared memory "serves only to restrict the possible traces of a process" and is "a modeling convenience". → https://pure.iiasa.ac.at/3067/1/XB-88-401.pdf / primary `[F8]`
- **A23. The DAI canon itself treats task-sharing, synchronization and functional accuracy under partial failure as the core problem** (Smith & Davis; Smith CNP; Yang/Huhns/Stephens; Lesser & Corkill FACS; Durfee/Lesser/Corkill coherent cooperation; Rosenschein plan synchronization — order and pagination verified via Crossref on *Readings in DAI*, Bond & Gasser 1988). / primary (bibliographic metadata) `[F8]`

### B. LLM-MAS: topology, roles, memory, credit (F9)

- **B1. No topology wins universally; the winner is task-dependent.** MacNet (chain/tree/graph × 6 sub-topologies, MMLU/HumanEval/SRDD/CommonGen-Hard): "a chain topology is more suitable for software development, while a tree topology is ideal for creative writing … no single topology consistently excels across all tasks." / https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07155 / primary `[F9]`
- **B2. Density helps on average; IRREGULAR beats REGULAR.** MacNet: dense mesh > tree > chain on average, yet "despite the intuitive appeal of densest interactions (i.e., mesh), they do not always yield optimal performance. In contrast, **irregular topologies often demonstrate statistically significant advantages**" (hypothesised cause: information overload); random topologies consume ≈51.92% less time than mesh. / same URL / primary `[F9]`
- **B3. Direction matters — divergent > convergent.** MacNet: "reversing the directions of specific topologies can lead to significant performance degradation. Typically, **divergent** topologies … substantially outperform their convergent counterparts"; recommendation: "maximize divergence while minimizing convergence". / same URL / primary `[F9]`
- **B4. Star and tree topologies can measurably FAIL to beat a single agent.** AgentPrune, 5 gpt-4 agents, MMLU: Vanilla 82.14; Chain 82.35 (+0.21); **Star 80.79 (−1.35)**; **Tree 81.89 (−0.25)**; Layered Graph 78.41 (−3.73); Complete Graph 83.15; Random Graph 83.76; GPTSwarm 83.98; DyLAN 80.16 (−1.98). "certain topologies, such as star/tree structures, fail to consistently improve performance … even resulting in performance drops of 0.17% ∼ 3.97%." / https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02506 / primary `[F9]`
- **B5. Pruning edges beats dense topologies on BOTH cost and quality.** AgentPrune: 28.1%–72.8% token reduction; MMLU $5.6 vs $43.7 for SOTA topologies; GSM8K AgentPrune-C 95.62 (+10.22), AgentPrune-R 95.83 (+10.43) vs Vanilla 85.40; GPTSwarm+AgentPrune MMLU prompt tokens 3,055,230 → 990,312 (32.4%), $47.60 → $23.05, accuracy 83.98 → 83.05. Pruned graphs recover a role structure (Critic many incoming edges, Knowledge Expert many outgoing). / same URL / primary `[F9]`
- **B6. Communication topology is LEARNABLE and the learned topology ≈ task structure.** GPTSwarm (REINFORCE over DAGs): Mini Crosswords 0.465 → 0.575 (10 iters) → 0.668 with node optimization; on MMLU the optimized swarm 0.8301 at $1.82 vs DyLAN 0.8366 at $14.99. / https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16823 / primary `[F9]`
- **B7. Dynamic topology + dynamic team composition is the best-supported combination, and beats static topology and single-agent.** DyLAN: "10.2% improvement to LLM Debate in terms of accuracy, with 10.6% lower #API calls"; on GPT-4-0613 code gen DyLAN 92.1 Pass@1 (+3.7) at 15.94 API calls vs MetaGPT 85.9 at >30 calls, AgentVerse 89.0 at 27, LATS 94.4 at >40, Reflexion 91.4 at 7.32. Ablation: removing team reformation 82.9 → 76.2 Pass@1 (critical); removing early stopping 7.15 → 13.00 API calls (efficiency only). / https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02170 / primary `[F9]`
- **B8. Role LABELs are near-inert; MEASURED CONTRIBUTION and the INTERACTION PATTERN are what pay.** MachineSoM (3-agent societies, easy-going vs overconfident traits, MATH/MMLU/Chess): "distinct societies composed of 3 agents possessing varied traits play an indistinctive role in shaping performance" (all significance tests insignificant; unchanged at 2–10 agents); what matters is the thinking-pattern permutation and uniform patterns within a round. / https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02124 / primary `[F9]`
- **B9. Measured contribution beats human priors beats random.** DyLAN Table 13 (7 candidates, 5 MMLU subjects, GR): no optimisation 63.5; Random 64.8; **Human Prior 66.7**; **Agent Importance Score 73.6** (+10.1). Code gen: 76.2 (23.04 calls) / Random 75.6 / Human Prior 78.0 / AIS 82.9 (16.85 calls). "In High School Statistics, Clinical Knowledge, and Public Relations subjects … prior selection performs even worse than random selection." / same URL as B7 / primary `[F9]`
- **B10. The best PROPOSER is not the best AGGREGATOR.** MoA, AlpacaEval 2.0 LC win rate, 2 layers, Qwen1.5-110B aggregator: as aggregator 110B 61.3 vs WizardLM-8x22B 52.9; as proposer WizardLM 63.8 vs 110B 56.7. "WizardLM demonstrated excellent performance as a proposer model but struggled to maintain its effectiveness in aggregating responses." / https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04692 / primary `[F9]`
- **B11. Sub-agent capability beats orchestrator capability; heterogeneous mixing is model-family-dependent.** "In centralized architectures, configurations with high-capability sub-agents outperform those with high-capability orchestrators across all model families"; mixing helps Anthropic (+31%) but DEGRADES OpenAI and Gemini. / https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296 / primary `[F9]`
- **B12. Role prompts are a cheap, high-yield intervention.** MAST: "improving agent role specifications alone yields a **+9.4%** success rate increase for ChatDev with the same user prompt and LLM (GPT-4o)"; separately "ensuring the CEO had the final say contributed to a +9.4% increase" (the paper uses 9.4% twice — see Conflicting evidence). Disobeying role spec is FM-1.2, 1.5% of failures. / https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657 / primary `[F9]`
- **B13. Coordination protocol classes each carry a named cost.** MAC survey: Static — "relies on accurate initial design and domain knowledge", "Fixed channels may deal with scalability"; Dynamic — "higher resource usage due to real-time adjustments"; Centralized — "if the central node fails the entire system might collapse"; Decentralized — "inefficient resource allocation"; Role-based — "rigid structure … performance relies on agents' connection le[vel]". / https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.06322 / primary `[F9]`
- **B14. A FLAT SHARED MEMORY STORE LOSES TO NO MEMORY AT ALL.** CoMem Table 1, PDDL under DyLAN: G-Memory 59.53 vs no-memory 67.78 (−8.25); MetaGPT 52.80 (−14.98); MemoryBank 50.92 (−16.86); Voyager 58.17 (−9.61). Named mechanisms: "noise accumulation" and "**behavioral homogenization**" (role-specific trajectories averaged out, lim H(π_ai‖π_aj) = 0). / https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15009 / primary `[F9]`
- **B15. Private + curated-shared beats either alone.** CoMem ablation (ALFWorld/PDDL): AutoGen private-only 83.58/62.41, collective-only 85.07/65.10, both 88.31/74.44; DyLAN 80.25/59.17, 77.61/67.22, both 87.31/69.50. "removing the collective layer leads to a 12.03-point drop … while removing the private layer causes a 9.34-point drop … the private layer providing a slightly stronger safeguard against memory pollution." Depth/width are inverted-U optima (k=3 or 5; K_max=5/6; EMA α=0.5). / same URL / primary `[F9]`
- **B16. Per-agent PRIVATE memory beats centralized shared memory.** DecentMem (per-agent dual-pool): best average accuracy in 14 of 15 (backbone × framework) cells; avg +8.6% over G-Memory, +26.1% over no-memory, up to +52.5%. / https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22721 / primary `[F9]`
- **B17. Naive share-all HURTS accuracy; an admission policy fixes it.** Learning to Share (GPT-5.1, three parallel teams): AssistantBench no-shared 24.0/1389s, **share-all 23.0/784s**, LLM-filtered 25.7/856s, learned selective 26.7/882s; GAIA 47.9 → 44.2 (share-all) → 49.1 (learned selective). "naively admitting all intermediate steps … yields reduced runtime but a **drop in task accuracy**". / https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05965 / primary `[F9]`
- **B18. Team count is non-monotone even with shared memory.** LTS, controller not retrained, GAIA memory-free baseline: K=2 23.0/1161s, **K=5 25.4/2034s, K=10 22.4/3411s** (more teams HURT); LTS 28.5/828s, 31.6/1234s, 29.1/2521s. / same URL / primary `[F9]`
- **B19. More agents HELPS for independent sampling + voting, on hard tasks, with weaker models.** Agent Forest: GSM8K +12–24%, MATH +6–10%, MMLU +5–11%, HumanEval +4–9%; 15 samples of Llama2-13B reach 59% GSM8K > Llama2-70B 54%. / https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05120 / primary `[F9]`
- **B20. …but it FAILS when agents must read each other.** Same paper: "two notable exceptions are observed when integrated with the debate method with the Llama2-13B and Llama2-70B models, which result in failed cases. This failure in performance is attributed primarily to the **noise generated by referencing the answers of other agents** during the debate process." Also gains are non-monotone in difficulty (best at I=100–200, taper at I=400). / same URL / primary `[F9]`
- **B21. Budget-aware compute beats fixed compute.** Adaptive Inference-Time Compute: "74% of the improvement from using 16 samples can be achieved with only 1.2 samples on average"; "50-75% of samples can be pruned early in generation with minimal degradation"; AlpacaEval 21% → 34% with 16 samples, GSM8K 84% → 91%. / https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02725 / primary `[F9]`
- **B22. Layered aggregation beats a single round and beats a ranker.** MoA: 3 layers/6 proposers 65.1% ±0.6 AlpacaEval 2.0 LC win rate vs GPT-4 Omni 57.5%; 2 layers 59.3%; "significantly outperforms an LLM-ranker baseline". - **B23. Identical agents are the classic failure; diversity is the fix.** GPTSwarm: with all agents prompted identically, "we observe no score improvement over the single agent baseline"; with 7 role-differentiated agents the optimized swarm improves 2.1% ±1.1%. MoA: "an LLM tends to generate better responses when presented with outputs from other models, even if these other models are less capable by itself". / https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16823 , https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04692 / primary `[F9]`
- **B24. The one LLM-native credit-assignment mechanism is a STUB: identical credit to every action.** Agent Lightning: "In our current implementation, LightningRL simply assumes each action within the episode has the same value, equal to the final return R"; richer credit is explicitly future work. LLM-as-credit-critic works — but the evidence is MARL, not LLM-MAS.** "Speaking the Language of Teamwork" (LLM generates dense agent-specific rewards from a natural-language task description + team goal) and LLM-MCA (centralized LLM reward-critic decomposing environment reward per agent) operate on numeric-policy MARL agents with numeric rewards, not natural-language role agents. / https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03723 , https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16863 / primary `[F9]`
- **B27. - **B28. LLM judges are order-biased — any peer-ranking/credit mechanism must control for position.** "the quality ranking of candidate responses can be easily hacked by simply altering their order of appearance"; Vicuna-13B beat ChatGPT on 66 of 80 queries with ChatGPT as evaluator. Fixes: Multiple Evidence Calibration, Balanced Position Calibration, Human-in-the-Loop. PRD adds self-enhancement bias in multi-agent settings, fixable by peer rank + peer discussion. / https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17926 , https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.02762 / primary `[F9]`
- **B29. AgentScope offers per-agent shared-vs-private as a CHOICE, plus a fault-tolerance ladder.** RAG agents may load a shared KnowledgeBank object OR a copy, and "a modification to an object may affect all the agents who use it"; fusion weights configurable because "knowledge may be of different importance or trustworthiness"; auto-retry with configurable max_retries; rule-based correction tools at "no LLM API call cost"; agent-level fault handling via memory module + message hub for "self-critique, pairwise critique, and human-augmented critique". / https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14034 / primary `[F9]` (platform guidance, no controlled experiment)
- **B30. A flat topic-keyed lease board with no dependency graph has no published counterpart.** DyLAN/MacNet/GoT use DAG edges as internal routing, never expose the dependency graph to agents as an inspectable artifact; MAC names it an open challenge. Unverified. / cross-file synthesis of F9 gaps / primary (absence) `[F9]`

### C. Failure evidence + measured coordination overhead (F10)

- **C1. MAST: 14 failure modes in 3 categories — Specification 41.77%, Inter-Agent Misalignment 36.94%, Task Verification 21.30%; category correlations low (0.17–0.32).** 150+ traces across 5 MASs; 1600+ annotated traces / 7 frameworks in the released corpus; inter-annotator κ = 0.88; LLM-judge annotator (o1 few-shot) accuracy 0.94, κ 0.77; generalization κ 0.79. / https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657 , https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v2 / primary `[F10]`
- **C2. The NAMED failure modes, with per-mode share (use F10's set — see Conflicting evidence C-1):** FM-1.3 step repetition **17.14%** (largest single mode; caused by *rigid turn configurations*, i.e. a topology property) · FM-2.6 reasoning-action mismatch **13.98%** · FM-2.2 fail to ask for clarification **11.65%** · FM-1.1 disobey task specification 10.98% · FM-1.5 unaware of termination conditions 9.82% · FM-3.1 premature termination 7.82% · FM-2.3 task derailment 7.15% · FM-3.2 no/incomplete verification 6.82% · FM-3.3 incorrect verification 6.66% · FM-1.4 loss of conversation history 3.33% · FM-2.1 conversation reset 2.33% · FM-2.4 information withholding 1.66% · FM-1.2 disobey role specification 0.50% · FM-2.5 ignored other agent's input 0.17%. / https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v2 §4.1 / primary `[F10]`
- **C3. ### D. Cross-cluster synthesis (what the three angles jointly imply for OUR plugin)

- **D1. The classic literature already names our two open mechanics as known, documented failures.** (a) No dependency ordering = the blackboard HEARSAY-I failure (shared state too coarse ⇒ "recomputed by each knowledge source" = duplicate work) and the contract net's *absent* `predecessors` field; Smith 1980 enforces ordering by withholding announcement. (b) No contention record = the contract net's *absent* structured refusal reasons (INELIGIBLE / BUSY / LOW RANKING / NO TEMPLATE). (c) No ownership/handoff = the contract net's *absent* manager/contractor record and termination message. (d) Idle waiters = Smith's efficiency criterion counting idle processors as loss. [F8]
- **D2. The modern literature says the flat room is the weakest shape, and the fix is not more agents.** Flat shared store < no-memory (B14); peer-to-peer = 17.2× error amplification (C14); shared-context/dependent tasks are where multi-agent does not pay (C17, C10); one flat log makes every agent re-aggregate every payload (C18–C19, C24). [F9][F10]
- **D3. The two tracks are complementary with a measured ordering.** MAST's own intervention table on one benchmark: prompt/roles +9.4% vs topology +15.6% (C5) ⇒ MECHANICS is the larger lever and GUIDANCE is the cheap prerequisite. [F8][F9][F10]
- **D4. Every headline number is conditional, and the condition is a usable rule.** Use the swarm only when the single agent is stuck (≈ below 45% on the subtask, C11) and the task is decomposable (C9/C10); keep injected coordination at ≈0.4 messages per agent-turn (C13); prefer divergent over convergent fan-out (B3); size teams by composition, not count (B9, C33). [F8][F9][F10]

## Cluster verdict

1. **The classic literature already specifies the mechanics our room lacks, so our open items are not novel risks but known classic failures**: dependency ordering enforced by *withholding the announcement* (Smith 1980 `predecessors`), ownership + termination + handoff as first-class contract records, and refusal-with-reason (INELIGIBLE / BUSY / LOW RANKING / NO TEMPLATE) as the precedent for a `contended` record kind — while Smith's own efficiency criterion already prices idle waiters as loss [F8].
2. **The modern literature says the flat room is the weakest shape, and the fix is NOT more agents**: a flat shared store loses to no-memory (59.53 vs 67.78) and homogenizes roles, peer-to-peer is the 17.2× error-amplification archetype (vs 4.4× with a validation bottleneck), and shared-context/dependent work is exactly where multi-agent does not pay (Anthropic first-party; PlanCraft −39% to −70%; overall mean MAS −3.5%) [F9][F10].
3. **MECHANICS outranks GUIDANCE on evidence but both are required**: MAST's own intervention table puts topology (+15.6%) above prompt/roles (+9.4%) on one benchmark, role *labels* are near-inert while proposer/aggregator and reviewer/writer separation pays, yet the one first-party account of duplicated work was fixed by delegation-boundary guidance rather than a mutex — so guidance must specify *structure and boundaries*, not personas [F8][F9][F10].
4. **Every headline number is conditional and the condition is the usable rule**: use the swarm only when the single agent is stuck (≈<45% on the subtask) and the task is decomposable; keep injected coordination at ≈0.4 messages per agent-turn; prefer divergent over convergent fan-out; size teams by composition (2 diverse ≈ 16 homogeneous), not count — there is **no** primary source establishing a universal optimum agent count [F8][F9][F10].
5. **Hard caveats to carry into the report**: six-plus of the brief's own citations are phantom and caught independently by all three workers (2401.06322, 2307.03062, 2401.00561, 2504.15710, 2509.09676, 2405.05522, "Single Agent to Explore", "LLM-Blender: …through Dialogue", "Revisiting LLMs-as-a-Judge", "Heylighen & Strogatz 2001 CSR 2(1)", "O'Hare & Riley AAAI-88"); MAST's per-mode decimals must come from F10's §4.1 read, not F9's set; and no source in this cluster supports the retrieval-over-coordination-literature track (no working credit assignment, no per-agent budget study, no escalation-efficacy measurement) [F8][F9][F10].
